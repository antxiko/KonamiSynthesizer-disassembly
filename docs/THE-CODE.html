The code

8,637 instructions in 1,187 routines, all with a name or a comment; 3,767 instructions carry a comment, 43.6%. The listing is generated from src/synth.notes and reassembles the ROM byte for byte.

Every frame

H.KEYI (0x4015) drives the program: the PSG effects, the keys (0x4817: the first one pressed as row × 8 + bit + 1, with repeat after 40 frames) and the screen's phase. In the intro, 0xD800 is the phase and 0xD801 the step, and every phase is a chain of djnz; in the program, 0xD8F4 is the screen and 0x4805 the table of the nine. SYN and PLAY disable interrupts and enable them when they finish.

SYN, the synthesizer

0x55F0 prepares and 0x5767 takes one turn per sample: the LFO, the three envelopes, the oscillator, the filter, the amplitude, and the byte to RAM.

  • The duration (0x5613): the longest of the three envelopes in samples, plus the amplifier's release, rounded up to a page; over 16 KB it is not computed. Every 1/128 of that duration there is a tick that moves the on-screen cursor (0x4BD9).
  • The envelopes (0x5829, 0x58D5, 0x5981) are one and the same state machine, the phase in a pointer: attack raises the level by 1 every A samples up to 255, decay lowers it every D down to S, sustain waits for all three to be in theirs, and release lowers it every R down to 0. A parameter at zero skips the phase.
  • The oscillator (0x5A4B) keeps a 16-bit phase that advances FEET × 256 per sample, plus or minus what the LFO and the oscillator's envelope say, and reads the wave at its high byte.
  • The filter (0x5B1C) is two one-pole stages with feedback: the cutoff is CUT plus what the filter's envelope and LFO add, and RES (0-8) picks the feedback and the gain in 0x5C12 and 0x5C1B. The output is the second stage (BPF), the first (LPF) or what is left of the input minus the first (HPF), according to byte 24 of the tone.
  • The amplitude (0x5C7E): the sample times the amplifier's LFO and times its envelope level.
  • The loop: when the oscillator and the filter have reached their sustain and the pointer falls on the start of a page, that page is noted in 0xDCFD (0x59D9); PLAY will repeat it while the key is held. With EG TYP on DUR there is no loop.

tools/motor.py is this code written anew in Python, with its 8- and 16-bit arithmetic and its roundings; tests/test_motor.py checks that the sixteen tones come out byte for byte the same as what the Z80 writes in openMSX.

PLAY, the pitch

Three tables of thirteen routines (0x5E8B with a loop page, 0x6709 without, 0x6EBB for listening in WAVE EDIT). Every key has in 0xD230 a routine r and a delay D (0x5DA9, and 0x5E29 per note), and the routine is always the same thing: the byte to the DAC, D turns of a wait loop, a look at whether the key is still down, and padding of instructions that do nothing (nop, cp 0, neg, inc ix…).

Counted instruction by instruction, within each routine every path costs the same per sample, and routine r takes 59 + r + 14 × D cycles of the Z80's data sheet: the thirteen routines are a one-cycle fine adjustment. On an MSX every opcode fetch carries a wait state, and what is measured in openMSX is 67 + 16 × D for routine 0 (In the emulator). While the tail of the sample plays, the keyboard is scanned row by row, and another key starts another note: PLAY is monophonic.

FILE: the tape

On tape (0x4D04 saves, 0x4D8C loads), four blocks through the BIOS, each after its header:

  1. the type sixteen times: 0x21 TONE, 0x22 LAYOUT, 0x23 SAMP;
  2. the seven letters of the name, in the font's code (0x21 = A);
  3. the length in two bytes and, only in SAMP, the loop page;
  4. the data: TONE to 0xC000 (4,608 bytes), LAYOUT to 0xD200 (176), SAMP to 0x8000 (0x4E7A).

When loading, with a blank name any file of the type will do; otherwise it looks for the one with that name.

The ROM also carries disk routines through the Disk BASIC's BDOS (0xF37D: NAME.T, .K and .S, and a directory of up to 35 names, 0x4F4D), and a DISK option in the menu that is only drawn if there is a drive (H.PHYD, 0x438F). They have not been tested.

The tape that came with the cartridge carries seven SAMPs on its two sides: PIANO, BRASS and ORCHE of 16,384 bytes, TRUMPET of 12,288, SAMISEN of 7,792, DOG of 3,072 and CRASH of 16,384.

The printer

SHIFT F3 (0x52B7) dumps the screen as dots through LPTOUT, in 64 bands of four rows and 192 columns (0x5419), with the sequences at 0x54B6; from WAVE EDIT it also prints the table of the wave, 64 signed samples in eight rows (0x5516). From the code: it has not been tried on a printer.

Konami's Synthesizer was published by Konami for the MSX in 1986; its catalogue number is RC-741 and it is 32 KB with a DAC. All rights in the program remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
