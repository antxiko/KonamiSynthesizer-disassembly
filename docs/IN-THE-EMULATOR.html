In the emulator

The pictures are built from the ROM, not captured. openMSX is for checking those pictures against the real VRAM, and for measuring what the code says about the sound.

All with openMSX and the Philips_VG_8020 machine, one emulator at a time.

Checking the screens

tools/omsx_dibujo.tcl follows a key script and dumps the VRAM, the RAM from 0xC000 up and the VDP registers when told; tools/omsx_tone16.tcl does not go by the clock: it waits for the intro to be at the title, starts the program, opens TONE EDIT and dumps one screen per tone, going from one to the next with INS. tools/coteja_todo.py builds every screen with tools/pantallas.py and compares it with its dump, byte for byte in everything it writes and dot for dot in the picture.

Twenty-three screens. In seven of the TONE EDITs the dump catches the cursor in the other phase of its blink (colour 12 instead of 8, 0xA5AA): it is the only different byte and it is accepted.

The synthesizer

tools/omsx_sintesis.tcl notes every byte SYN writes at 0x57B0; tests/test_motor.py compares the sixteen tones with what tools/motor.py gives: byte for byte the same.

What reaches the DAC

tools/omsx_dac.tcl notes every byte the Z80 writes to 0x4000 with its instant: it synthesizes tone 1, gives the Z key routine 9 and delay 11 (A3) and holds it for half a second. tools/coteja_dac.py rebuilds what has to arrive and compares:

bytes to the DAC22,273: the rest value, the whole 16 KB and 23 more turns of loop page 0x84
different from motor.py0
period between writes254.37 cycles on average (254 and 255); sonido.py uses 254.4

tools/omsx_ritmo.tcl is the measurement the thirteen routines' cycles in sonido.py come from: each one with D = 32 and D = 64, 3,000 samples.

The sample in RAM

With a breakpoint after the write at 0x57B0: during SYN the slot register reads 0xF4 (page 2 is slot 3, the RAM) and the byte read back after writing it is the one written.

Konami's Synthesizer was published by Konami for the MSX in 1986; its catalogue number is RC-741 and it is 32 KB with a DAC. All rights in the program remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
