Findings

Each one says where it comes from: the code, a table in the ROM or a measurement in openMSX. The measurements are told in In the emulator.

A synthesizer inside the Z80

The cartridge carries no sound chip: it carries an 8-bit DAC at 0x4000, and the program is a subtractive synthesizer that computes the note sample by sample (0x5767): an oscillator with fourteen waves, a two-stage resonant filter, an amplifier, three ADSR envelopes and an LFO with sample and hold. It writes it all into the 16 KB at 0x8000 and then plays it.

Measured in openMSX: tools/motor.py, the engine written anew, gives the sixteen factory tones byte for byte the same as those the Z80 writes at 0x57B0.

The pitch is a loop of cycles

PLAY does not change the frequency of anything: it sends the sample to the DAC byte by byte and waits between one and the next. Every key has one of thirteen routines and a delay D (0x5DA9), and the thirteen routines are the same loop with different padding of instructions that do nothing. Counted, routine r costs 59 + r + 14 × D cycles per sample: the thirteen are a one-cycle fine adjustment.

Measured in openMSX: with routine 9 and D = 11 (A3), 254.37 cycles on average between writes to the DAC, 254 and 255; with the MSX's M1 wait state that is 67 + 16 × D for routine 0. And it is monophonic: one key at a time (0x5EA5).

The sample lives in the computer's RAM

The cartridge has no RAM. Before synthesizing, playing or loading a SAMP, 0x4105 puts at 0x8000-0xBFFF the RAM slot it noted at start-up (0xD854), and 0x410D brings the cartridge back, which is where the screens' graphics are. RAM at 0x8000 is needed: a 16 KB MSX has none.

Measured in openMSX: during SYN page 2 is slot 3 (A8 = 0xF4) and every byte written stays.

The noise comes from the BIOS

Oscillator wave 16 and the LFO's sample and hold are not a table: 0x5AD4 reads from 0x0000 onwards, the BIOS ROM, rotates each byte and accumulates it. The noise depends on the computer: tone 14 (PARC1) sounds different on every machine, and tools/sonido.py needs the first 256 bytes of a BIOS to make it.

The DAC starts without a thump

The first interrupt routine (0x4118) only raises the DAC from 0 to 0x80, one step per interrupt, and at the 129th it hands the hook to the per-frame one. From the code.

TONE, LAYOUT and SAMP

Three tape files, with the length in front and, in SAMPs, the loop page: TONE is the sixteen tones with their sixteen waves (4,608 bytes), LAYOUT the note of every key with its routine and its delay (176), and SAMP the whole sample (up to 16 KB). From the code, and the tape that came with the cartridge confirms it: seven SAMPs, from 3,072 to 16,384 bytes. The ROM also carries disk routines (.T, .K and .S through the BDOS) and a DISK option that is only drawn if there is a drive (H.PHYD); they have not been tested.

The second page of the menu

SHIFT changes the bottom menu (0x48B2): F1 copies a tone and its wave onto another, F2 lists the sixteen, F3 prints the screen, and F4 is LAYOUT, where every key of the music keyboard gets the note you choose (0xBBA4); CTRL and HOME bring back the factory one. From the code: these four screens are not built from the ROM.

The attack does not save its phase

In the three envelopes, on entering the step-by-step attack (0x5849, 0x58F5, 0x598F), the program loads the phase's routine into HL and does not store it in the pointer, unlike in the decay and the release. It works the same, because it re-enters from the top every sample; they are three three-byte ld hl that do nothing.

What nobody calls, and the mark

Ten pieces of code nobody calls: a jp behind the header (0x4012), a graphics loader driven by a six-byte table at 0x5800 that does not exist (0x49CD), stray copies of FILE and printer routines (0x520A, 0x522A, 0x54A4), a search in a list (0xBCA2)… And 0xB834, which only runs from its copy in RAM.

At 0xBF62 the ROM says RC741 86 5 31, and in its last ten bytes it carries Konami's hidden mark: シンセサイザー, RC-741. The format of that mark was discovered by Manuel Pazos, and he is thanked for it; tools/marca_konami.py reads it.

Konami's Synthesizer was published by Konami for the MSX in 1986; its catalogue number is RC-741 and it is 32 KB with a DAC. All rights in the program remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
